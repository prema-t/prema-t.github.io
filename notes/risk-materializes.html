Morpho Curated Vaults: When Risk Materializes

My curiosity is about what happens when perceived risk becomes reality, particularly where human judgment and protocol mechanisms meet.

I first looked at the basic role structure of a Morpho curated vault: the depositor, curator, allocator, sentinel, and the underlying Morpho markets. That helped me understand the different roles, their authority, and where decision-making power sits.

My current question is what happens when those risk judgments prove incorrect.

A depositor supplies assets to a vault but does not directly choose every underlying market that receives the capital. The decision about where that capital goes is delegated. The curator defines much of the vault’s risk framework, while the allocator determines how capital is distributed across approved markets within those constraints. Other roles, such as the sentinel, have narrower powers designed to respond to particular risk conditions.

Although the underlying Morpho markets are permissionless lending markets, a curated vault therefore introduces another layer of judgment between the depositor and those markets. The depositor is exposed not only to risks associated with borrowers, collateral, liquidity, interest rates, and liquidation mechanics, but also to the quality of the vault’s risk decisions.

That raises several questions I do not yet have complete answers to.

One is how changes to risk parameters propagate through the vault. If a curator changes a market cap, removes a market, or changes the vault’s permitted exposure, how quickly does that affect where depositor capital is actually allocated?

A second question concerns stress conditions. I understand the basic role of liquidation at the underlying market level, but I still want to understand how those mechanisms behave during severe market movements and how liquidity conditions affect the outcome.

The question I am most interested in is what happens when the curator or allocator misjudges the risk. A vault might, for example, allocate too much capital to a market whose collateral later becomes unstable, whose oracle behaves unexpectedly, or whose liquidity deteriorates.

The protocol itself may continue functioning exactly as designed, yet depositors may still experience losses because the judgment applied around the protocol proved inadequate for the conditions that emerged.

That distinction matters.

Smart contracts determine what actions are possible and enforce the rules of the market. A curated vault, however, adds another layer: human or organizational judgment about which markets are acceptable, how much capital should be allocated to them, and when exposure should be reduced.

So the next question I want to investigate is:

When risk materializes inside a Morpho curated vault, where does the loss ultimately land, and what mechanisms, if any, exist to protect the depositor?
